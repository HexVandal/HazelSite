There's a specific kind of quiet that only exists after midnight. Not silence —
the refrigerator is doing its thing, some car goes past every twenty minutes,
my playlists have gotten progressively stranger. But nobody is texting. Nobody
needs a reply. The day is over and tomorrow hasn't started making demands yet.

That window is when I actually get things done.

## On the playlist

Right now it's the same seven songs on loop. I've read that this is either
comfort-seeking behaviour or a sign of an unwell mind, and I've decided not to
look into which one applies to me.

There's something about a song you know perfectly — every breath before the
chorus, every bad edit — that lets your brain stop paying attention to the
music and start paying attention to the work. New music is a full-time job for
the ears.

## Things I've noticed at 3 AM

- Every idea sounds profound. Almost none of them survive daylight. Save the
  draft anyway; the 2% that does is worth the other 98%.
- Problems look smaller. Something that felt like the end of the world at noon
  is a line of code at 3.
- The urge to redesign everything is strongest around 3:40. Do not open Figma.
- If you're still awake past four, you're not productive anymore, you're just
  tired with a side of optimism.

## The trade

I know it's not healthy. I've read the articles. But there's a version of
"healthy" that involves being asleep at 11 PM and useless until noon, and I've
tried both, and only one of them writes anything down.

So: coffee after 6 PM, notes app open, headphones on. The best part of the day
happens while everyone else is unconscious. It's the one appointment I never
miss.
