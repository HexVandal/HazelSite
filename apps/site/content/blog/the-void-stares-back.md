A friend once asked why I keep a personal site when social media gets a
thousand times the reach. Fair question. I gave a pretentious answer about
owning my words, and then I thought about it properly and came up with a better
one.

## Reach is not the same as audience

A post that gets four hundred likes and zero replies is a broadcast. A post that
gets read by six people who actually know what you're talking about is a
conversation. I've had better conversations in blog comment sections than in
every group chat combined.

The internet made publishing free and attention expensive. That trade
punishes everybody who has something specific to say and rewards everybody
who has something broad to say. A personal site is a way of opting out of that
deal quietly.

## Writing as thinking

The real reason is less noble. I don't know what I think until I've written it
down badly, deleted it, and written it again. Posts are just the receipts.

- If an idea survives being typed, it might be real.
- If it only works out loud, it's a vibe.
- If it needs a whiteboard, it's a hobby.

## Permanence is a feature

Everything on the timeline evaporates in 72 hours. Everything on a domain
sticks around until the bill comes due. That's a small, stubborn kind of
honesty: if I believe something, it can live somewhere with my name attached,
where I can't quietly edit it after the fact without you noticing.

```txt
the void is not empty
it is just quiet
```

So — this is me, shouting into it anyway. If you found this, hi. There's more
where that came from.
